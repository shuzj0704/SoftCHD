# Abstract

Manipulating deformable objects remains a significant challenge in robotics due to their high-dimensional, non-rigid, and underactuated dynamics. In this work, we tackle the highly dynamic task of twirling a handkerchief—a representative example of continuous, agile manipulation of soft materials—using a robotic arm. We leverage Isaac Lab to construct a physically accurate deformable model of the handkerchief and simulate realistic interactions between the manipulator and the fabric. Building upon this simulation environment, we train a reinforcement learning policy that enables the robot to autonomously learn the complex coordination required to sustain stable handkerchief twirling over extended time horizons. Our results demonstrate that the agent successfully acquires this intricate skill without explicit demonstrations, highlighting the potential of model-free reinforcement learning for high-dynamic deformable object manipulation. This work advances the frontier of soft object control and provides a foundation for more dexterous and robust fabric manipulation in real-world applications.
